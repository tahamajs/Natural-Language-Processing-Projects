Logistic Regression outperforms Naive Bayes on every metric. The largest practical gain appears in precision and F1-score, suggesting fewer false positives while preserving strong detection of spam emails.

\subsection{Uncertainty-Aware Metric View}

To avoid over-interpreting point estimates from a single split, we estimated non-parametric bootstrap confidence intervals (500 resamples) for F1-score:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Logistic Regression F1 95\% CI: [0.9182, 0.9579]
    \item Naive Bayes F1 95\% CI: [0.8795, 0.9277]
\end{itemize}

The intervals overlap only partially and the center of the Logistic Regression interval remains higher, supporting the robustness of the observed ranking.

\subsection{Ranking Quality and Error-Profile Signals}

Using prediction probabilities from the from-scratch Logistic Regression implementation, we also computed ranking-centric metrics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item ROC-AUC: 0.9876
    \item Average Precision (PR-AUC proxy): 0.9565
\end{itemize}

These values confirm that the model remains strong across thresholds, not only at the default decision point.

To inspect failure modes, we summarized dominant token patterns among errors. False positives were associated with terms such as \texttt{milwaukee}, \texttt{club}, \texttt{fares}, and \texttt{coupon}, while false negatives were associated with terms such as \texttt{financial}, \texttt{session}, \texttt{conference}, and \texttt{security}. This suggests two practical directions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item augment lexical models with contextual sequence cues to distinguish legitimate business email from promotional language;
    \item incorporate threshold calibration conditioned on business-vocabulary density.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why Logistic Regression Wins Here}

Three factors explain the observed superiority of Logistic Regression:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Correlated lexical features:} words in real emails are not conditionally independent, violating Naive Bayes assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Discriminative optimization:} Logistic Regression optimizes a direct classification objective.
    \item \textbf{Feature normalization and gradient training:} stable optimization improves calibration on dense high-dimensional vectors.
\end{enumerate}

Naive Bayes still offers advantages in simplicity and training speed, but in this setting accuracy-oriented performance favors the discriminative model.
